# STAR Method

The STAR method provides a structured way to answer behavioral interview questions.

## Framework

| Letter | Stands For | What to Cover |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | **Situation** | Set the scene — project, team, context, timeline |
| **T** | **Task** | Your specific responsibility or challenge |
| **A** | **Action** | What *you* did (use "I", not "we") |
| **R** | **Result** | Quantified outcome — what happened because of your actions |

## Tips

- **Quantify results** whenever possible: "reduced latency by 40%", "saved 3 hours/week", "increased test coverage from 60% to 90%".
- Spend most of your time on **Action** (the bulk of the answer).
- Keep each story to **~2 minutes**.
- Prepare **6–8 stories** that can flex to cover multiple question types.
- Use different stories for different questions (don't reuse the same example).

## Situation Starters

- "At [Company], I was working on [Project]…"
- "During my role as [Title], our team faced…"
- "While leading [Project], we encountered…"

## Result Starters

- "As a result, we reduced X by Y%."
- "This led to a 2× improvement in…"
- "The feature was launched on time and received…"
- "I received positive feedback from the manager and the change was adopted org-wide."

## Common Question → Recommended Story Type

| Question | Look for a story about… |
|---|---|
| Tell me about a challenge you overcame | Technical or interpersonal conflict |
| Describe a time you failed | Real failure + learning + recovery |
| Tell me about a project you're proud of | High impact, complex work |
| Tell me about a conflict with a colleague | Disagreement + resolution |
| Describe a time you influenced without authority | Persuasion, data-driven argument |
| Tell me about a time you took initiative | Self-directed improvement |
